# Myco Quest Art Direction (Professional Quality Baseline)

## Vision

Myco Quest should look like a modern SNES-era fantasy RPG: readable pixel art, rich atmosphere, and clear visual storytelling. Every scene must feel deliberately composed, not procedurally bland.

## Primary style anchors

- **Silhouette clarity:** player, enemies, bosses, interactables, and portals must be recognizable at a glance.
- **Tile grammar discipline:** water edges, cliff transitions, roof patterns, and biome details should look authored.
- **Pixel integrity:** no anti-aliased blur, no debug outlines, no unstyled rectangle placeholders.
- **Cinematic framing:** title screens, bosses, and key quest beats should use layered depth + atmosphere.

## Region visual language

- **Neon Mushroom Forest:** deep violets, cyan glow, magenta accents, luminous spores, thick canopy layers.
- **Rougarou Swamp:** purple marsh tones, toxic greens, fog strips, wet terrain highlights.
- **Chainlands / corruption zones:** cooler obsidian palette with rune glow and constrained high-contrast lighting.

## Composition rules

- Use three layers minimum in important scenes:
  1. background silhouette layer,
  2. playable midground,
  3. foreground accent layer (foliage, particles, architecture trims).
- Keep camera focus area readable; do not bury gameplay elements in VFX noise.
- Keep UI panels out of the center play area whenever possible.

## Sprite and environment quality bar

- Avoid repeated flat blocks for major props.
- Include micro-variation for terrain (checker, dither, bevel, or accent motifs).
- Houses/caves/castles/towers should read as unique destinations, not recolored duplicates.

## Animation guidance

- Prioritize responsive gameplay over long decorative animation.
- Use subtle loops for atmosphere (fireflies, fog drift, glow pulse).
- Combat hits should have immediate impact feedback (flash, shake, pop-up damage text).
